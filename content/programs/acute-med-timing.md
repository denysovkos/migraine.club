## Why try this

Taking acute migraine medication early — within 30 minutes of attack onset — is associated with better response and shorter attacks in observational data. Late dosing can lead to incomplete relief and the temptation to redose, which raises the risk of medication-overuse headache (MOH).

## What we'll measure

- **Adherence**: percentage of attacks where you logged an acute dose within the target window after onset.
- **Outcomes**: average time-to-dose during the program, plus headache days before vs. during.
- The program runs silently — no daily prompts. We compute the delta between attack start time and the first acute dose you log.

## How it works

1. You set your target window. Default is 30 minutes after onset.
2. When you log an attack and an acute dose, we measure the gap.
3. Each attack becomes a hit, miss, or "na" (no dose taken).
4. After 6 weeks we'll show your adherence and a conservative outcome comparison.

## A note on MOH risk

This program is **not** about taking more acute meds. Earlier dosing within an attack often means a single dose is enough — fewer redoses, lower MOH risk over time. If your existing acute-med-days are already approaching the MOH threshold (~10 per month for triptans, ~15 for simple analgesics), discuss with your doctor before starting.

## How to stop

Pause or end the program any time from Programs settings.
